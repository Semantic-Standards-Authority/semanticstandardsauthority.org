SSA AUTHORITY DECLARATION (SSA‑AD1)
Semantic Standards Authority

1. Institutional Identity
The Semantic Standards Authority (hereinafter – SSA) is an independent, non‑commercial, and neutrality‑driven institutional body established to define, maintain, and safeguard semantic standards, semantic integrity, and semantic interoperability across domains, systems, and jurisdictions.

The SSA operates as a neutral semantic authority, providing clarity, structure, and authoritative interpretation for concepts, definitions, semantic artefacts, and institutional language.

2. Foundational Authority
The authority of the SSA derives from:

the semantic act of establishment,

the institutional mandate defined in the SSA Charter,

the public function of maintaining semantic coherence,

the jurisdiction of semantic standards,

and the responsibility to ensure interoperability between conceptual systems.

The SSA’s authority is not commercial, not political, and not subordinate to private interests.

3. Purpose of the Authority
The SSA exists to:

define and maintain semantic standards,

ensure semantic interoperability between institutions, systems, and documents,

prevent semantic ambiguity, drift, or misuse,

provide authoritative definitions and interpretations,

establish semantic protocols,

and uphold the integrity of institutional language.

4. Scope of Jurisdiction
The SSA exercises authority over:

semantic definitions,

conceptual frameworks,

institutional terminology,

semantic validation processes,

and cross‑domain semantic interoperability.

The SSA does not regulate content, opinions, or political positions — only semantic structure and clarity.

5. Institutional Neutrality
The SSA is:

independent,

non‑aligned,

non‑ideological,

and committed to semantic objectivity.

Its decisions are based solely on semantic correctness, coherence, and interoperability, not on external pressures or interests.

6. Semantic Act of Authority
By this declaration, the SSA affirms:

its existence as an institutional authority,

its mandate to define and maintain semantic standards,

its responsibility to ensure semantic clarity,

and its jurisdiction over semantic interoperability.

This declaration constitutes the formal activation of the SSA’s semantic authority.

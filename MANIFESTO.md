SSA MANIFESTO (SSA‑M0)
Semantic Standards Authority

1. Purpose
The Semantic Standards Authority (SSA) exists to ensure clarity, stability, and interoperability within semantic structures.
The SSA recognises that modern systems, institutions, and digital infrastructures depend on precise and unambiguous meaning.
Where meaning collapses, systems collapse.
Where semantics drift, interoperability fails.
Where definitions fragment, institutions lose coherence.

The SSA is established to prevent such failures.

2. Foundational Principles
2.1 Neutrality
The SSA is neutral.
It does not serve political, commercial, ideological, or interest‑driven agendas.
Its only mandate is semantic correctness and semantic integrity.

2.2 Clarity
Ambiguity is a systemic risk.
The SSA maintains clarity as a foundational requirement for all semantic artefacts.

2.3 Stability
Semantic structures must be stable over time.
Stability ensures continuity, reliability, and institutional trust.

2.4 Interoperability
Semantic artefacts must function across domains, systems, and jurisdictions.
Interoperability is a prerequisite for modern digital and institutional ecosystems.

2.5 Accessibility
Semantic standards must be publicly accessible, transparent, and open.

3. Semantic Authority
The SSA asserts authority over:

definitions

vocabularies

ontologies

classifiers

semantic schemas

conceptual frameworks

institutional terminology

This authority is not coercive.
It is structural: grounded in correctness, coherence, and necessity.

The SSA does not regulate opinions, content, or ideology.
It regulates meaning.

4. Semantic Integrity
Semantic integrity requires:

precise definitions

consistent usage

controlled evolution

documented changes

prevention of semantic drift

authoritative interpretation when ambiguity arises

The SSA maintains semantic integrity as a public good.

5. The Problem of Semantic Drift
Semantic drift is a systemic failure mode.
It occurs when:

terms lose precision

definitions diverge

institutions use inconsistent language

systems interpret concepts differently

meaning becomes unstable

The SSA exists to counteract drift through authoritative semantic governance.

6. The Role of Standards
Semantic standards:

define meaning

ensure interoperability

prevent fragmentation

provide shared reference points

enable coherent communication between systems and institutions

Standards are not optional.
They are structural requirements for functioning systems.

7. Institutional Responsibility
The SSA accepts responsibility to:

define semantic standards

maintain them

publish them

archive them

interpret them

revise them when necessary

deprecate them when required

This responsibility is continuous and non‑delegable.

8. Public Mandate
The SSA serves the public interest by ensuring:

semantic clarity

semantic stability

semantic interoperability

semantic accessibility

The SSA’s work is open, transparent, and globally relevant.

9. Commitment
The SSA commits to:

neutrality

precision

transparency

stability

interoperability

accessibility

long‑term stewardship of semantic structures

This Manifesto is the philosophical and normative foundation of the SSA.

10. Entry Into Force
This Manifesto enters into effect upon its publication on semanticstandards.uk and forms the conceptual basis for all SSA documents, standards, and institutional actions.

SSA CHARTER (SSA‑C0)
Semantic Standards Authority

I. PREAMBLE
The Semantic Standards Authority (hereinafter – SSA) is an independent, non‑commercial and neutrality‑driven institution established to ensure the clarity, stability, and interoperability of semantic structures, vocabularies, ontologies, classifiers, and other semantic artefacts.

This Charter defines the legal status, operational principles, competences, document hierarchy, governance structure, and procedural framework under which the SSA performs its functions. The Charter is the highest‑level internal legal instrument of the SSA and is binding upon all SSA bodies, members, and participants.

The Charter is grounded in the SSA Manifesto, which sets out the philosophical, conceptual, and normative foundations of the institution. The Charter shall not contradict the Manifesto and shall be interpreted in accordance with the principles expressed therein.

The SSA operates as Semantic Standards Authority Limited (Company No. 17178828), registered in the United Kingdom, while its scope of activity is international and independent of any state, organisation, or commercial entity.

This Charter enters into force upon its publication on semanticstandards.uk and is recognised as the authentic institutional legal norm of the SSA.

II. INSTITUTIONAL STATUS
The SSA is a neutral authority responsible for the stewardship, clarification, and governance of semantic standards and semantic artefacts.

The SSA is non‑commercial and shall not engage in profit‑seeking activities.

The SSA is independent from governments, corporations, political entities, and interest groups.

The SSA’s mandate is global, and its standards, recommendations, and decisions apply internationally.

The SSA may cooperate with public, private, academic, and civil organisations, provided such cooperation does not compromise its neutrality or independence.

III. PURPOSE AND FUNCTIONS
The SSA shall:

Establish, maintain, and publish semantic standards, vocabularies, ontologies, and related artefacts.

Ensure semantic clarity, consistency, and interoperability across domains and systems.

Provide authoritative interpretations of semantic structures and resolve semantic ambiguities.

Maintain a transparent and publicly accessible repository of standards and institutional documents.

Develop and maintain procedures for the creation, revision, and deprecation of semantic artefacts.

Serve as a neutral arbiter in semantic disputes between organisations, systems, or communities.

Promote the adoption of open, interoperable semantic practices.

IV. DOCUMENT HIERARCHY
The SSA recognises the following hierarchy of institutional documents:

SSA Manifesto – foundational philosophical and normative document.

SSA Charter – institutional legal framework (this document).

Authority Declarations – binding interpretations and institutional positions.

Standards – normative semantic specifications.

Technical Notes – non‑binding explanatory or advisory materials.

Operational Procedures – internal procedural rules.

In case of conflict, documents higher in the hierarchy prevail.

V. GOVERNANCE STRUCTURE
The SSA is governed by the Authority Council, the institution’s highest decision‑making body.

The Authority Council shall:

adopt and amend standards,

issue Authority Declarations,

interpret the Charter,

oversee institutional integrity.

The Executive Secretariat manages operational, administrative, and publication functions.

Expert groups may be established to develop or review standards, but they hold no independent authority.

All governance bodies must operate transparently and publish their decisions.

VI. DECISION‑MAKING
Decisions of the Authority Council shall be made by consensus whenever possible.

When consensus cannot be reached, decisions may be adopted by a qualified majority of two‑thirds.

Authority Declarations require consensus.

All decisions must be published and include rationale and references to relevant documents.

VII. STANDARDISATION PROCESS
Any individual or organisation may submit proposals for new standards or revisions.

Proposals undergo:

initial review,

expert evaluation,

public consultation,

final adoption by the Authority Council.

Standards must be versioned, dated, and archived.

Deprecated standards remain publicly accessible with clear status indicators.

VIII. TRANSPARENCY AND PUBLIC ACCESS
All SSA documents shall be publicly accessible on semanticstandards.uk.

Drafts, consultations, and decision records must be published.

No document may be classified or restricted unless required for security or legal compliance.

IX. AMENDMENTS TO THE CHARTER
Amendments may be proposed by any member of the Authority Council.

Amendments require:

public notice,

consultation period,

adoption by consensus or a three‑quarters majority.

Amendments enter into force upon publication.

X. ENTRY INTO FORCE
This Charter enters into force upon its publication on semanticstandards.uk and remains valid until amended or replaced in accordance with Section IX.

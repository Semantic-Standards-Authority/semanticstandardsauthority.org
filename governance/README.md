Governance Framework
Semantic Standards Authority (SSA)

Overview
The governance structure of the Semantic Standards Authority (SSA) ensures neutrality, transparency, and stability in the development, maintenance, and interpretation of semantic standards.
This document provides an overview of the SSA’s institutional bodies, decision‑making processes, and governance principles.

1. Governance Bodies
1.1 Authority Council
The Authority Council is the highest decision‑making body of the SSA.
It is responsible for:

adopting and amending standards

issuing Authority Declarations

interpreting the SSA Charter

overseeing institutional integrity

approving governance procedures

The Council operates on the principles of neutrality, transparency, and semantic correctness.

1.2 Executive Secretariat
The Executive Secretariat manages operational, administrative, and publication functions.
Its responsibilities include:

maintaining repositories and documentation

coordinating consultations and reviews

ensuring procedural compliance

publishing standards, declarations, and decisions

The Secretariat does not hold independent decision‑making authority.

1.3 Expert Groups
Expert Groups may be formed to support the development or revision of standards.
They:

provide technical analysis

prepare drafts

review proposals

support interoperability assessments

Expert Groups are advisory only and cannot issue binding decisions.

2. Decision‑Making
2.1 Consensus
The SSA prioritises consensus as the primary decision‑making method.

2.2 Qualified Majority
If consensus cannot be reached, a two‑thirds majority may adopt:

standards

revisions

procedural rules

2.3 Authority Declarations
Authority Declarations require full consensus due to their binding interpretative nature.

3. Transparency
The SSA operates under strict transparency requirements:

all decisions are published

all standards and drafts are public

all governance procedures are documented

consultations are open and archived

No governance action may be taken in secrecy unless legally required.

4. Procedural Documents
The governance directory may include:

decision records

procedural rules

consultation archives

meeting summaries

implementation notes

These documents ensure institutional continuity and accountability.

5. Amendments
Governance documents may be amended by the Authority Council following:

public notice

consultation period

adoption by consensus or qualified majority

All amendments must be published.
